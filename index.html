Hear the Warming — 27 Years of Global Temperature in 60 Seconds

NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 — The Earth Information Jukebox. One note per month, January 1999 to October 2026. Higher anomaly, higher pitch. Months that stand out from their 25-month neighbourhood play louder.

Gold dots: the 33 "surprise" months (rolling z-score ≥ 2.0, 25-month window). Data: NASA GISTEMP v4, global land-ocean monthly anomaly vs 1951–1980, in °C. Source: GLB.Ts+dSST.csv.

  • January 2007: +1.01 °C

  • 2019 – early 2020: Mar 2019 – Feb 2020, up to +1.23 °C
  • Record heat 2023: Mar and Jul–Nov 2023, incl. +1.48 °C (Sep 2023)
  • 2026: Aug +1.42 °C, Oct +1.55 °C — highest in the series

Honest limits

The detector flags deviations in either direction, so it also catches cold dips (Jan 2008, Dec 2010, Feb 2021). Because the series trends upward, a rolling z-score flags many recent points; 33 moments is too many to narrate one by one, so the clusters above are named instead. The pitch scale is a listening choice, not a physical scale.

Method: plain Python, standard library only, rolling z-score. No proprietary code. Code, data and audio are open source (Apache 2.0).
