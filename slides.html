The idea

One note per month. A higher temperature anomaly plays a higher pitch. In 60 seconds you hear the planet warming — no chart to read.

334 months · January 1999 – October 2026 · NASA GISTEMP v4

2 / 6

How it works

  • Data: NASA GISTEMP v4, global land-ocean temperature index, monthly anomaly vs the 1951–1980 baseline.
  • Surprise: a rolling z-score (25-month window, threshold z ≥ 2.0) flags 33 months out of 334. Flagged months play louder, with a brighter note (a fifth above).
  • Built with plain Python, standard library only. No proprietary code, no third-party model.

3 / 6

What we heard

  • El Niño 2015–16: October 2015 – March 2016, up to +1.36 °C (Feb 2016).
  • Record heat 2023: March and July–November 2023, including +1.48 °C (Sep 2023).
  • October 2026: +1.55 °C — the hottest month in the entire series.

4 / 6

Why it matters — and the honest limits

27 years of warming is a slow curve that eyes glaze over. Hearing it makes the change immediate, and it works for people who cannot read a chart.

Limits: the detector flags deviations in either direction, so it also catches cold dips (Jan 2008, Dec 2010, Feb 2021). The pitch scale is a listening choice, not a physical scale.
